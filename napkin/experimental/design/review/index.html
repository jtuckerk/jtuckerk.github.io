Interaction review

A review of how napkin is used on a phone and with a mouse, with proposals for Tucker to rule on. Nothing here is built. The full text, with costs and the bug list, is in docs/plans/interaction-review.md.

Pictures with a solid border are screenshots of the app today. Pictures in a dashed frame marked sketch are drawings of a proposal, not the app.

What to change first

  1. bug Two units that cannot be added show an error on the node. Today the app goes blank and stays blank.
  2. unreachable A tap selects a number, a node, or an edge. One bar at the bottom holds its actions.
  3. clash You drag sideways on the selected number to change it. Two fingers always zoom.
  4. missing There is undo and redo.
  5. better Changing an input changes it at once. Apply and Cancel are only for a computed value.
  6. hidden The choices in a what-if are a list at the bottom, with names, numbers, and units.
  7. better On a phone, napkin opens to the list, with a line to type in and a "+".
  8. better Numbers show enough digits to be true, and a NaN always has a reason.

The gestures, before and after

The rule after: a tap selects, and the selected thing shows what can be done to it. A drag means what it starts on. Two fingers always mean the canvas.

Bug first: units that cannot be added blank the app

Type 3 m + 2 s and press Enter, or connect 3 m and 2 s to a + by hand. The preview says the units do not fit, and then the app goes blank. The workspace was saved, so a reload is blank too.

1. A tap selects, and one bar holds the actions unreachable today

After. A tap on a number, a node, or an edge gives it a clear outline and does not open the keyboard. A bar at the bottom shows it in large type with its actions. Tap the name to rename, the number to type, the unit for the unit list.

Today. Rename, lock, digits, and delete are behind a hover. On a phone the buttons show only because a tap sends a hover, and they are 22 px. A tap on a number opens the keyboard and shows the full number in a field too narrow for it.

Why not the alternatives. Bigger buttons on each node cover neighbours and shrink with zoom. A menu on long press is hidden. A side panel has no room on a phone. Spreadsheet apps on phones work the proposed way: one tap selects, a second tap edits.

Cost. Medium, about three days. Interface only. It removes the hover buttons.

2. Drag sideways on the selected number; two fingers always zoom clash today

After. With a number selected, one finger or the mouse on it, dragged sideways, changes it. Right is more. A pinch always zooms. The wheel changes a number only when it is selected.

Today. After a tap on a number, a pinch changes that number in place of zooming. In the test a pinch moved commute from 12 to 15. With a mouse, turning the wheel to zoom changes any number under the pointer, by 1/100 of the digit it shows, so 12 becomes 12.03 and still reads 12.

Why not the alternatives. The two-finger scrub is the cause of the clash and cannot be found by looking. Up and down clashes with scrolling the list. Figma, Blender, and Bret Victor's Tangle all change a number by a sideways drag.

Cost. Medium, two days. Interface only. It removes the two-finger scrub.

3. What-if choices are a list at the bottom hidden today on a phone

After. Changing a computed value opens a short list in the bar at the bottom. Each row is one input that could move, with its name, old number, new number, and unit. A lock on the row means "never move this", and the row goes away.

Today. Each choice is a chip beside its node. On a phone the chips for commute and workdays are under the next node, and two more are off the screen. A chip has no name and no unit. The first pick was the unnamed 2.

4. Undo and redo missing

After. An undo button in the top bar, and Cmd+Z. Every change to the workspace can be taken back.

Today. None. With a node selected, Backspace deletes it at once. The hover × deletes at once. An applied what-if cannot be taken back.

Why. Undo lets the rest be direct: no confirm on delete, and no Apply on a plain change. Cost. Medium, two days: keep the last saved documents in memory and load the one before. No change to the core model.

5. Errors and empty things say what is wrong broken today

After. An empty operation shows two empty slots and no number. A result with no real answer shows a dash and the reason. A failed save shows "Not saved" until a save works.

Cost. Small, one to two days. A small core change: an operation gives a reason when it cannot compute.

6. A "+" and a line to type in at the bottom hidden today

After. A "+" at the bottom right opens a full-width line above the keyboard, with the reading of the entry over it. After Enter the view moves to show the result. The same line works in the list.

Cost. Medium, two days. Interface only. The preview stays as it is.

7. Changing an input changes it at once could be better

After. A change to a typed number flows through the graph and is done. Apply and Cancel show only for a change to a computed value. To hold a result still, lock it. The "Advanced Mode" setting goes away.

Cost. Small to medium. Needs a check that the core offers the same choices when a result is locked. Best after undo.

8. Numbers show enough digits to be true could be better

After. A result shows three figures. A value moved by a what-if shows enough digits to differ from before. One scrub step is one of the last digit shown, and never coarser than the second figure: 5 goes 5.1, 5.2.

Cost. Small, one to two days. The default for results is one line.

9. A phone opens to the list could be better

After. A narrow screen opens to the list. A switch in the top bar goes to the canvas and back, and is remembered. Each group shows inputs first and results after. Locked values fold into one row. The menu lists workspaces first.

10. A new node never covers another could be better

After. A node added from a handle takes the nearest free place below its source. A node added anywhere moves aside if it would cover another. The view moves to show it. The ring at a handle opens below the handle.

Cost. Small, one to two days. Canvas layout only.

11. A new person sees a real example and three hints could be better

After. The first run opens a small real workspace, fitted to the screen, with three hints that go away once done. An empty workspace shows one line of help.

12. An operation's header reads as the formula could be better

After. The header names each input, or shows its number with its unit: commute * 2, 5,520 mile / mpg. During a what-if it shows the numbers on screen. Today it shows bare numbers, and they do not change during a what-if (see the desktop what-if picture above). Cost. Small, half a day.

Small things

  • The preview repeats "(in the workspace)" after every name. Use colour.
  • Text in every field at least 16 px, so iOS does not zoom; number fields ask for the number keyboard.
  • Apply and Cancel 44 px tall, above the home bar.
  • A selected bar's controls stay inside the screen on a phone.

Judged fine as it is

  • The text box's preview of how an entry is read.
  • The ring, and the plot's place on it.
  • The unit list.
  • The bar and the plot: tabs, dragging a tab, controls on selection.
  • The what-if in the list, and the highlights on the canvas.
  • Auto save, and tapping the workspace name to rename it.

Open questions

  1. Should the two-finger scrub go? Recommended: yes, once the sideways drag is in.
  2. Should changing an input ever wait for Apply? Recommended: no. Drop Advanced Mode; lock a result to hold it.
  3. Should a phone open to the list or the canvas? Recommended: the list, with a one-tap switch.
  4. Which examples should a new device get? Recommended: a new small commute example first; keep Gas vs EV; stop seeding the two test graphs.
  5. How many digits should a result show at first? Recommended: three.
  6. Should the bar at the bottom be the same on a desktop? Recommended: yes, with hover kept only as a preview.
